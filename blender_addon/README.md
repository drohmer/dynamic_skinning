# Blender add-on — Dynamic Skinning (prototype)

Adds dynamic skinning (oscillations and drag) to a rigged mesh in Blender (4.4 and later, tested with 4.4 and 5.2). The mesh oscillates when its bones move: when a bone is manipulated in Pose Mode, and during the playback of the animation. This is the simple version of the method, without delay (dLBS) or flexible origins.

## Installation

Download [`dynamic_skinning-0.1.1.zip`](https://github.com/drohmer/dynamic_skinning/releases/download/blender-addon-v0.1.1/dynamic_skinning-0.1.1.zip), or build it from the repository:

```bash
blender --command extension build --source-dir blender_addon/dynamic_skinning --output-dir .
```

Then, in Blender: *Edit > Preferences > Get Extensions > ▾ > Install from Disk…* and choose `dynamic_skinning-0.1.1.zip`.

## Example files

With the extension installed, open a file of `blender_addon/examples/` and start the playback (Space):

- **`cylinder.blend`**: the cylinder of the applications; the end bone rotates by 90°, pauses, and comes back.
- **`spot.blend`**: Spot the cow (`blender_addon/assets/spot/`, texture packed in the file), with the animation of the applications: the head nods, then turns to each side, and the ears oscillate.

The dynamic skinning restarts by itself when the file is opened. The armature is in Pose Mode: a bone can also be rotated by hand (R). Without the extension, the mesh is displayed without dynamic skinning.

The files are generated by `blender -b --factory-startup --python blender_addon/tools/make_examples.py`.

## Use on your own models

1. Select a mesh with an Armature modifier (the vertex groups are named after the bones).
2. Click *Enable* in *Properties > Object > Dynamic Skinning* (with the mesh or its armature selected), or in *3D View > Sidebar (N) > Dyn. Skinning*. Both panels contain the same settings.
3. Switch the armature to Pose Mode and rotate a bone (R): the mesh follows with a lag, then oscillates.

Parameters (notations of the paper): frequency **f** (Hz), damping **τ** (s) and amplitude **A** of the kernel κ(t) = A exp(−t/τ) sin(2π f t), drag **D**, and bend magnitude **γ**. An optional `DS_bend` vertex group gives the per-vertex bend weight, which can be painted; without this group, it is 0.5 everywhere, as in the applications.

*Reset* recomputes the weights after a change of the rig or of the mesh, and stops the oscillations.

## How it works

- **`core.py`**: the method, with numpy and without dependency on Blender (same computations as `code/dynamic_skinning_source/`): hierarchical weights β_ij, angular velocity of each bone measured from its local rotation, history at a fixed time step Δt = 1/60 s (independent of the frame rate), convolution with κ, then bend.
- **`blender_link.py`**: reads the rig and the pose, computes the LBS positions, and writes the offset of each vertex in the `ds_offset` attribute of the mesh. A *Dynamic Skinning* Geometry Nodes modifier, added at the end of the stack (after the Armature), adds it to the positions. A 60 Hz timer does the update.
- **`__init__.py`**: properties, operators and panels.

The bend angle is computed on the model scaled to [−1, 1] (half size of the mesh at rest): the result does not depend on the scale of the model in Blender, and the parameter values are those of the applications.

Cost of an update (Python and numpy in float32, on the development Mac, without the re-evaluation of the modifier by Blender, which takes about 4 ms for 100k vertices): cylinder with 2 bones, 0.2 ms for 1,440 vertices, 1.1 ms for 25,600, 3.9 ms for 102,400; Spot (26 bones), 0.6 ms for 2,930 vertices, 6.6 ms for 70,274 (subdivided). The LBS is computed bone by bone (one matrix product per bone, on the vertices it influences), and the bend uses ‖ω × u‖ = ‖v‖ and the cross product in matrix form.

## Limitations of the prototype

- The time is the real time: the result of a playback depends on the playback speed, and there is no "bake" yet for a reproducible final render.
- The LBS is recomputed with numpy (no Dual Quaternion, no shape keys before the Armature).
- No delay (dLBS), no flexible origins, no GPU version.

## Content of the directory

- **`dynamic_skinning/`**: the extension (the only installed part, and the content of the zip).
- **`examples/`**: the example files.
- **`assets/spot/`**: the model of Spot (copied from `code/assets/spot/` of the applications). The add-on is self-contained: neither the extension, nor the examples, nor the tools use the rest of the repository.
- **`tools/`**: generation of the examples (`make_examples.py`), with the construction of the rigged cylinder (`make_cylinder.py`) and a small importer of the asset format of the applications (`make_spot.py`: `mesh.obj`, `skeleton_*`, `rig_joint`, `rig_weights`, `texture.png`), reusable for other models.
- **`listing/`**: page of the extension on the Blender Extensions Platform: description (`description.md`), icon (256x256), featured image and comparison (1920x1080). The images are rendered from `examples/spot.blend` with the installed extension (`render_spot.py`, Workbench), then composed by `compose.py` (Pillow, NumPy).
- **`tests/test_cylinder.py`**: non-regression test without interface. The bone of the cylinder rotates by 90° in 0.7 s; the test prints the maximal offset at a few instants and saves Workbench renders:

```bash
blender -b --factory-startup --python blender_addon/tests/test_cylinder.py -- <directory>
```
